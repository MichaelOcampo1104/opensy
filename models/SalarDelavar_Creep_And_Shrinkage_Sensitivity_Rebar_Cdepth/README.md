# SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Sensitivity_Rebar_Cdepth

**Purpose:** The sensitivity of the creep and shrinkage response of a 2D reinforced-concrete frame to the column rebar diameter (18 to 32 mm) and the column depth (500 to 600 mm): 36 cases, each analysed over 5 years of analysis time with Hysteretic reinforcement. For each case the model gives the largest value over the 73 load steps of the base shear, axial force, moment, displacements, rotation and stiffnesses; the bilinear fit of the base shear (ductility, over-strength factor, R and the stiffnesses); and the rebar ratios. This is the last of the three sub-models in the source folder `CONCRETE_FRAME_EXAMPLES/CREEP_AND_SHRINKAGE`. The first two are `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis` and `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Period_Analysis`.

**Building System:** A single-storey, single-bay reinforced-concrete portal frame with both bases fixed. The columns are 3 m high and 500 mm wide, with a depth from 500 to 600 mm and 50 mm cover. Each column has six bars, three on each face, of a diameter from 18 to 32 mm. The beam spans 7 m, is 500 mm wide and 300 mm deep, with 50 mm cover and eight bars whose diameter is 4 mm smaller than the column's. The concrete has a strength of 25 MPa, with the confinement ratio 1.2 for the core in both sections and an unconfined cover.

**Model Description:** 2D model with three DOFs per node, five nodes and four corotational `nonlinearBeamColumn` elements (the two columns, and the beam in two halves). Each section is a fibre section with 20 fibres along the depth and 5 across the width. The core and cover concrete are TDConcrete (ACI 209R-92 creep and shrinkage, with the source's constants), and the reinforcement is Hysteretic, the only steel model in this sub-model. The top-node loads are on a Linear time series, so the load factor equals the analysis time (Note 1). Each case applies the loads at day 28, switches creep on, and runs 73 load steps of 25 days to day 1853, with one ODB step per load step, the fallback ladder where needed (Note 3), and the fit of the base shear. The source runs its 36 cases in order: the rebar diameters outside and the column depths inside. The visualisation stages are written for the last case, whose curves are plotted.

**Results:** Over the 36 cases the largest base shear is 3,706 N and the largest axial reaction 3.706 × 10^7 N, in every case (the reference loads at day 1853; Note 1). The largest displacement in X runs from 1.17 mm (32 mm rebar, 600 mm depth) to 2.19 mm (18 mm, 500 mm), and the largest base moment from 1.43 × 10^7 to 2.68 × 10^7 N·mm. The rebar ratio RO_COL runs from 0.0068 (18 mm, 600 mm) to 0.0257 (32 mm, 500 mm). The bilinear fit of the base shear gives an elastic stiffness from 574 to 868 N/mm, a ductility ratio from 1.47 (28 mm, 600 mm) to 2.02 (18 mm, 500 mm), an over-strength factor from 5.24 (28 mm, 600 mm) to 6.10 (20 mm, 510 mm), and R = Ω × μ from 7.69 (28 mm, 600 mm) to 12.05 (18 mm, 500 mm). The last case (32 mm, 600 mm) has ductility 1.5039, over-strength 5.4635 and R 8.2167. The table of all 36 cases is `output/CONCRETE_FRAME_CREEP_AND_SHRINKAGE_SENSITIVITY_REBAR_Cdepth_RESULTS.csv`.

**Validation:** `output/validation.txt` ends with ALL CHECKS PASS. It checks the time sequence 28 + 25n in all 36 cases; the equilibrium of the X force, the Y force and the moment at every step of every case, with relative residuals of 1.2 × 10^-10, 1.7 × 10^-12 and 1.2 × 10^-12 (tolerance 10^-9); that every step converged; that the table is finite; that R = Ω × μ and RO_COL = 8 A_bar / (Hc Bc) in every case; the 76 values of the four corner cases of the source's table (18 mm and 32 mm rebar at 500 mm and 600 mm depth, 19 response columns each) to 10^-9; and the last case's X fit, and its Y fit against the source's printed values, to four decimals. The report also gives the last case's Y-fit R both ways: 19.56 with R = Ω × μ, and 6.45 with the source's formula, which the source prints (Note 2). Outside the repository, all 36 cases of the source's table agree with the converted table in every column, with a largest relative difference of zero. The analysis at the creep age does not converge in 10 of the cases (Note 3).

**Notes:**
1. **The load grows with time (not changed).** As in `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis`, Note 1. The source's loads are on a Linear time series, and the load factor equals the analysis time (28 + 25n), so the applied loads reach 1853 times their stated values at day 1853. The concrete passes its compressive strain limit well before that (Note 9). The source's results are reproduced as written, and a sustained load would change them: that is your decision.
2. **The source's Y fit.** The source's bilinear fit of the base axial force (Y) uses the ductility coefficient `(2 mu - 1)^0.5 / mu^0.5` in place of `mu`, so its R for the Y fit is not the R of the X fit. Its print of the Y ductility is `YY[2]/YY[1]`, where YY is the axial force, not the ductility (the same label error as LESSON 102). The converted model uses R = over-strength x ductility for both fits. The Validation gives the Y fit's R with the source's formula for comparison.
3. **Solver: an exception to the SmartAnalyze rule, approved on 2026-11-27.** As in `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis`, Note 3. Each step is one `ops.analyze(1)` call with the source's fallback ladder (`ANALYSIS_FUNCTION.py`, ported as `analyze_step`). This is a load-controlled analysis, which SmartAnalyze cannot run (AGENT.md §3c), so it falls under the LoadControl exception in §3c and §10, which the audit (item 22) allows only with documented approval. The user approved this exception on 2026-11-27. The source does not test its analysis at the creep age, and the converted model does not either. In 10 of the 36 cases that analysis does not converge (return code −3), and the load steps then start from its unconverged state, as in the source; with that behaviour kept, the sweep reproduces the source's table. Adding a ladder there, as the creep model does (its LESSON 117), changed those 10 cases by up to about 7 % in the over-strength factor and 13 % in R. The source's behaviour was kept on review, so the converted model follows the source at the creep age.
4. **Bilinear fits.** The source fits the base shear of each case inside its analysis (X: the base shear against the node-3 displacement), and it fits the last case again after the loop, in X and in Y. The table uses each case's own X fit (ductility, over-strength factor, R and the three stiffnesses). The last case's Y fit is in the Validation.
5. **Rebar ratios.** The source computes `RO_COL = 8 A_bar / (Hc Bc)` and `RO_BE = 6 A_bar / (Hb Bb)`, as ratios (not percentages). The column has six bars and the beam eight, so each ratio counts the other member's bar number, as in the confinement folder (LESSON 100). The converted model keeps the source's formulas, and the table's rebar columns follow them.
6. **Surfaces.** The source interpolates the 36 values onto a 100 x 100 grid (cubic) for its ten 3D figures. The converted `vis_12_sensitivity_surfaces.html` draws the 36 cases on their own 6 x 6 grid, without interpolation. Its correlation heatmap (`vis_13_correlation_heatmap.html`) is the source's `PLOT_HEATMAP`, on the same table.
7. **Outputs.** The source writes an Excel file, which needs openpyxl, and the project environment does not have it. The converted model writes the same table as `output/CONCRETE_FRAME_CREEP_AND_SHRINKAGE_SENSITIVITY_REBAR_Cdepth_RESULTS.csv`, with the source's column names. Each case has its ODB (nodal responses, tags 1 to 36). The figures of the last case are `vis_08_curves_last_case.html` and `vis_09_history_last_case.html`, and its deformed shape is `vis_05_deformed_last_case.html`. The model of the last case is written to `CONCRETE_FRAME_CREEP_AND_SHRINKAGE_SENSITIVITY_REBAR_Cdepth.json`.
8. **Source plots.** The source's logarithmic axes on its two stiffness plots are not reproduced, and its matplotlib figures are HTML files here. Its `PLOT_2D_FRAME` is not called; the deformed shape comes from `vis_defo`.
9. **Solver messages.** TDConcrete prints `Strain Compression Limit Exceeded` for each fibre beyond its compressive limit, from the first loaded steps on. This run printed 9,963,555 of these lines. They are reports from TDConcrete, not errors, and the run raised no exception.
10. **Not carried over.** The commented uniform load (`dl`, whose `eleLoad` is commented out), the commented recorder block and the stress-strain files it would write, the `TDConcreteEXP` settings (its material is commented out), the strain and stress values that no material call uses (`eps1C`, `fc2C`, `EtC`, `Bs`, `Esh`), the Steel01 branch (not used by this sub-model's loop), and the Excel file name. `py_ref/` keeps all the source's Python files.
11. **Source header.** The source's docstring describes a pushover analysis with cyclic degradation, which the code does not run. The converted header describes what the code runs.
12. **Time.** The source's `TIME` counts 25-day steps from zero. The analysis time is 28 + 25n. The last case's histories use the analysis time.
