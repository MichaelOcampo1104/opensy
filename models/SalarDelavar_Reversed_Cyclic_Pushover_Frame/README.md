# SalarDelavar_Reversed_Cyclic_Pushover_Frame

**Purpose:** Reversed cyclic pushover analysis of a 2D reinforced-concrete frame with the source's two reinforcement models (Steel01, and Hysteretic with hardening and ultimate strain), under the source's displacement protocol of 60 cycles of 500 samples (Note 1). At each of the 30,000 steps the model gives the base shear, the axial force, the base moment, the displacements, the rotation, the drift and the secant stiffnesses, and it builds the moment-rotation, shear-drift and P-M relations of the source's figures. This is the source's `CYCLIC_PUSHOVER` example, converted to the standard layout.

**Building System:** A single-storey, single-bay reinforced-concrete portal frame with both column bases fixed. The columns are 3 m high and 500 × 500 mm, with 50 mm cover and six 25 mm bars (three on each face). The beam spans 7 m and is 500 mm wide and 300 mm deep, with 50 mm cover and eight 18 mm bars (three on each outer layer and two in the middle). The confined core is 27.6 MPa and the cover 18 MPa (the source's values), and the reinforcement yields at 4000 MPa.

**Model Description:** A 2D model with three DOFs per node: four nodes (two bases and two top corners) and three corotational `nonlinearBeamColumn` elements (two columns and the beam), each with five integration points. Each section is a fibre section with 20 fibres along the depth and 5 across the width, with Concrete01 for the core and the cover, and Steel01 or Hysteretic bars. The control is displacement control in X at the top right node (node 4), driven by the source's reversed cyclic history, applied as the source's increments (Notes 1, 2 and 4). The reference load is 1 N in X and −1 N in Y at each top node, and the displacement control sets its load factor. Each step is one SmartAnalyze step (Note 3).

**Results:** Steel01 run: 30,000 steps; peak |displacement X| 410.6716 mm (step 29,750); peak |base shear| 1.764018 × 10^6 N; final displacement −3.917918 mm and final base shear 1.162091 × 10^6 N. Hysteretic run: 30,000 steps; peak |displacement X| 410.6613 mm (step 29,750); peak |base shear| 1.775197 × 10^6 N; final displacement −1.936256 mm and final base shear 5.868992 × 10^5 N. The per-step results are in `output/CONCRETE_FRAME_CYCLIC_RESULTS.csv`, with the source's column names and the step and the drift in percent added. The figures are `vis_08_curves_*.html` (P-M, shear-displacement, axial-displacement and moment-rotation), `vis_09_history_*.html` (the histories against the step) and `vis_05_deformed_steel01.html` (the deformed shape). The ODBs (`RespStepData-1.odb` and `RespStepData-2.odb`, with nodal responses every 60 steps) are in `output/`.

**Validation:** `output/validation.txt` ends with ALL CHECKS PASS. In both runs every one of the 30,000 steps returns success, with one analysis per increment. The axial and shear reactions balance the reference load (A + S = 0) to 3.4 × 10^-8 (Steel01) and 1.6 × 10^-7 (Hysteretic), and the moments about the origin balance, with the loaded nodes at their deformed positions, to 1.8 × 10^-8 and 6.9 × 10^-8 (tolerance 10^-6; Note 8). The source's printed values at steps 2, 3, 15,000 and 30,000 (increment, node-3 displacement and base shear), and the peak displacement and peak base shear of each run, are reproduced exactly to the printed digits. Outside the repository, against the source's full table of 30,000 steps, every column of both runs agrees with the source at every step with no difference, and no step of either run needed SmartAnalyze's fallback (its first attempt converged at every step).

**Notes:**
1. **The protocol is the source's, as written.** The source builds the amplitude of cycle i as i² × scale (scale = 80 mm / 60²) times sin(t), and it subtracts the previous cycle's whole array from each cycle before the arrays are concatenated. The increments therefore accumulate to a peak displacement of 410.7 mm, not the 80 mm that the source's `max_value` names. The converted model reproduces this. Changing the protocol would be the author's decision.
2. **The control node and the reported displacement.** The source calls `DisplacementControl` on node 3 and then on node 4. The second call replaces the first, so the control is node 4 in X. The displacements, the rotation and the drift are those of node 3, which the source reports, and they differ from the control: at step 2 the increment is 2.80 × 10^-4 mm and node 3 moves 2.59 × 10^-4 mm.
3. **Solver: SmartAnalyze with the source's test and algorithm.** The source sets no test before its loop. Its fallback ladder (`ANALYSIS_FUNCTION.py`) runs once in each run, at the first zero increment, and from then on the test is NormDispIncr with MAX_TOLERANCE 1e-6 and MAX_ITERATIONS 5000, and the algorithm is KrylovNewton with `-initial`. The converted model uses those settings for every step: `SmartAnalyze` with `testType="NormDispIncr"`, the tolerance and the iterations, and algorithm 100 (`UserAlgoArgs=["KrylovNewton", "-initial"]`). On a failed step, SmartAnalyze tries algorithms 10, 20, 30, 50 and 60, with relaxation 0.5 and a minimum step of 1e-4. That fallback replaces the source's ladder. Because the converted model runs SmartAnalyze throughout, AGENT.md §3c needs no exception here. SmartAnalyze's own default test (EnergyIncr at 1e-10) gave a step-2 base shear 1e-3 N away from the source, which is why the source's settings are used.
4. **The increments are passed unchanged.** Each increment goes to `StaticAnalyze` as it is, as the source passes `CYCL[step]` to `DisplacementControl`. An earlier version passed the difference between two cumulative targets (SmartAnalyze's `static_split`). That rounds each increment at about 10^-13 mm. The two versions first differed at step 1502, and the later steps then differed by up to about 2 × 10^-7 of the peak responses. With the increments passed unchanged, every step agrees with the source.
5. **Zero increments and secant stiffnesses.** The first increment is zero (the source's step 1), so the three secant stiffnesses there are 0/0. NaN is what numpy gives in the source, and `stiffness()` reproduces it, where Python floats would raise an error. Near the zero crossings, at steps 15,000 and 30,000, the displacements are about 10^-10 mm, so the secant stiffnesses divide by solver noise and reach about 10^12 N/mm. They are computed as the source computes them, and they are reproducible only as far as the displacements are.
6. **Excel becomes CSV.** The source writes an Excel file, which needs openpyxl, and the project environment does not have it. The converted model writes the same table to `output/CONCRETE_FRAME_CYCLIC_RESULTS.csv`, with the source's column names and the step and the drift in percent (the source plots the drift but does not export it).
7. **Figures.** The source's matplotlib figures are HTML files here. The source's logarithmic axes are not reproduced, and its `PLOT_2D_FRAME` is not called; the deformed shape comes from `vis_defo`. The source's PNG is kept in `py_ref/`.
8. **Equilibrium tolerance.** The source's test (and the converted one) bounds the displacement increments, not the force unbalance. The base reactions therefore balance the reference load only to about the displacement tolerance times the stiffness. The balance checks use MAX_TOLERANCE (10^-6), and the residuals are between 10^-8 and 2 × 10^-7. A check at 10^-9 would fail on the source's own solver settings.
9. **Not carried over.** The source's commented alternatives (the `Linear` and `PDelta` transformations and the commented plotting and printing statements) and its Excel file name. `py_ref/` keeps all the source's Python files. The source's header describes energy dissipation, degradation and collapse assessment in general terms; the converted model does not evaluate those statements.
