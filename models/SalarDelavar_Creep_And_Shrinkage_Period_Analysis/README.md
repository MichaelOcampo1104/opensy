# SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Period_Analysis

**Purpose:** The structural periods of the creep and shrinkage frame over 5 years of analysis time, for the two reinforcement models. At each of 73 load steps the model gives the four lowest periods of the structure, their smallest and largest values, the base reactions and displacements, and the three stiffnesses; it also fits a bilinear curve to the Steel01 run. This is the second of the three sub-models in the source folder `CONCRETE_FRAME_EXAMPLES/CREEP_AND_SHRINKAGE`. The first is `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis`, and the third, `CREEP_AND_SHRINKAGE_SENSITIVITY_ANALYSIS_REBAR_Cdepth`, is not converted yet (Note 13).

**Building System:** The same frame as `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis`: a single-storey, single-bay reinforced-concrete portal frame with both bases fixed. The columns are 3 m high and 500 x 500 mm, with 50 mm cover and six 25 mm bars. The beam spans 7 m, is 500 mm wide and 300 mm deep, with 50 mm cover and eight 18 mm bars. The concrete has a strength of 25 MPa, with a confined core (confinement ratio 1.2 in the column and 1.1 in the beam, Note 2) and an unconfined cover. Each top node carries a lumped mass of 10 N.s^2/mm, which is 10 t (Note 4).

**Model Description:** 2D model with three DOFs per node, five nodes and four corotational `nonlinearBeamColumn` elements (the two columns, and the beam in two halves). Each section is a fibre section with 20 fibres along the depth and 5 across the width. The core and cover concrete are TDConcrete (ACI 209R-92 creep and shrinkage, with the source's constants). The reinforcement is Steel01 (run 1) or Hysteretic (run 2), with f_y = 4000 MPa, E_s = 200,000 MPa and an ultimate strain of 0.12 for the Hysteretic steel (Note 8). The top-node loads are on a Linear time series, so the load factor equals the analysis time (Note 1). The analysis applies the loads at day 28, switches creep on, and runs 73 load steps of 25 days to day 1853. Each step has one analysis with the fallback ladder (Note 3), the base reactions and displacements, one ODB step, and an eigenvalue analysis of the four lowest modes (Note 5).

The largest period is the first period, a lateral sway mode. It grows by 16 % over the 5 years, as the creep softens the frame. The smallest of the four is a vertical mode of the top nodes, and it hardly changes. With the source's mass as written, the check run has the first period at 4.210742 s (day 53) and 4.887598 s (day 1853), and the smallest period at 0.416463 s and 0.416469 s. These are the source's printed values.

The four periods of the source's printed steps (check run) at day 53 are 4.2107, 0.5752, 0.4166 and 0.4165 s, and at day 1853 they are 4.8876, 0.5783, 0.4166 and 0.4165 s. The elastic check frame (gross sections, the same masses) has the periods 0.1458, 0.01965, 0.01420 and 0.01419 s with the SI mass. Its first two modes are sway modes in X, and its last two are vertical modes of the top nodes.

Bilinear fits of the Steel01 run, as the source plots them: in X (base shear against the node-3 displacement), elastic 640.5 N/mm, plastic 2008.6 N/mm and tangent 3753.0 N/mm, with ductility 1.784, over-strength factor 5.595 and R 9.984. In Y (base axial force against the node-5 displacement), elastic 1.0537 x 10^6 N/mm, plastic 1.2151 x 10^6 N/mm and tangent 1.2721 x 10^6 N/mm, with ductility 3.833, over-strength factor 4.420 and R 16.94. The static results are those of `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis`, because the mass does not enter the static analysis.

**Validation:** `output/validation.txt`, from the final run of this folder. All 52 checks pass, and the run log has no Python errors.
- **Time and equilibrium.** The 73 steps run from day 53 to day 1853, exactly 28 + 25n. At every step the base shear and axial force balance the applied loads, and the moments about the origin balance with the loaded nodes at their deformed positions (to 5.5 x 10^-11 or better, relative).
- **Periods.** At every step all periods are finite and positive, and the largest is never below the smallest.
- **Reproduction of the source.** The check run matches the source's printed displacements, base shear and axial force at steps 1, 2 and 73 (to 10^-9, relative). It matches the four printed periods at each of those steps to within 5 x 10^-5 s, which is the printed precision. Its fallback ladder is used at step 7 only, as in the source, and its bilinear fits match the printed values to 6 x 10^-6 (relative).
- **Eigenvalue pipeline.** On the elastic check frame (the same nodes, members and masses, with gross sections and E = 23,500 MPa), OpenSees's `-fullGenLapack` eigenvalues agree with numpy to 3.6 x 10^-14 (SI mass) and 3.1 x 10^-14 (source mass). The numpy values are computed by static condensation of the massless DOFs, which gives the finite eigenvalues exactly (Note 5). Eigenvalues also scale exactly as 1/mass, to 1.2 x 10^-14.
- **Mode shapes.** On the elastic check frame, the first two modes are X-dominated (sway), and the last two are Y-dominated (vertical) (Note 7).
- **Fallback ladder.** The main runs need it only for the analysis at the creep age, which converges on its first attempt (Note 3).

**Notes:**
1. **The load grows with time (not changed).** This is the same as in `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis`, Note 1. The source's loads are on a Linear time series, and the load factor equals the analysis time (28 + 25n), so the applied loads reach 1853 times their stated values at day 1853. The concrete passes its compressive strain limit from day 203 (Note 11). The source's results are reproduced as written, and a sustained load would change them: that is your decision.
2. **Beam confinement ratio.** As in `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis`, Note 2. The source passes Kfc = 1.1 for the beam, but its section function sets `Kfc = 1.2` and ignores the argument. The converted beam uses the named 1.1, and the check run keeps 1.2 and reproduces the source (LESSON 116 of the creep model). At day 53 the first period differs from the mass-only scaling by 0.1 % (the validation INFO line: the ratio of the two periods is 0.031658, against 0.031623 from the mass alone).
3. **Solver: an exception to the SmartAnalyze rule, approved on 2026-11-27.** As in `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis`, Note 3. Each step is one `ops.analyze(1)` call with the source's fallback ladder (`ANALYSIS_FUNCTION.py`, ported as `analyze_step`). This is a load-controlled analysis, which SmartAnalyze cannot run (AGENT.md §3c), so it falls under the LoadControl exception in §3c and §10, which the audit (item 22) allows only with documented approval. The user approved this exception on 2026-11-27. The source does not test its analysis at the creep age, so the converted model applies the same ladder to it.
4. **Mass units (AGENT.md §12j).** The source writes `MASS = 10000.0` with the label [kg], and uses it as written. In the N-mm-s system that is 10000 N.s^2/mm per node, which is 10^7 kg, and the first period is then 4.21 s. The converted model reads the label: 10000 kg / 1000 = 10 N.s^2/mm per node (1 N.s^2/mm = 1000 kg). This is the same reading as in the CQC model, which you confirmed. The periods scale with the square root of the mass, so the reading multiplies every period by 0.0316, and the static results do not depend on the mass. The first period is 0.133 s with the converted mass. You confirmed this reading. The check run keeps the source's value and reproduces the source's periods.
5. **Periods.** The source's `EIGENVALUE_ANALYSIS` calls `ops.eigen('-fullGenLapack', 4)` after each step and takes the periods as 2 pi / sqrt(lambda). The converted model makes the same call, and it returns the four periods in descending order, because the eigenvalues are in ascending order. The massless rotations are handled by the solver, and the check against static condensation (Validation) shows no error on this frame. AGENT.md §12h-2 advises against `-fullGenLapack` for models with stiffness contrasts, and this frame's check does not show one.
6. **The source's period figure pairs two runs.** The source plots the Steel01 displacements (its run 1) against the periods of the Hysteretic run (its run 2). It discards the periods of run 1 (`_, _` in its call), so the x and y of the figure come from different analyses. The converted model plots each run's periods against its own displacement (`vis_10_period_steel01.html` and `vis_10_period_hysteretic.html`). The source's x label says [m], but its values are in mm.
7. **Smallest and largest.** The source's `PERIOD_MIN` is the smallest of the four computed periods, and its `PERIOD_MAX` is the largest. Here the smallest is a vertical mode of the top nodes (about 0.0132 s), and the largest is the first sway mode. The converted columns keep the source's names (`PERIOD_MIN`, `PERIOD_MAX`).
8. **Reinforcement model.** The Steel01 and Hysteretic runs give identical periods (to 10^-12, relative) and displacements (to 10^-8). The steel barely yields, so the reinforcement model does not change these results. The source's ultimate strain for the Hysteretic steel is 0.12. In `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis` it is 1.504, but its Hysteretic results are the same, for the same reason.
9. **Source header.** As in `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis`, Note 6. The source's docstring describes a pushover analysis with cyclic degradation, which the code does not run. The converted header describes the analysis that the code runs.
10. **Time column.** As in `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis`, Note 7. The source's `TIME` counts 25-day steps from zero. The analysis time is 28 + 25n, and the converted CSV uses it (`TIME_DAY`).
11. **Outputs.** The source writes an Excel file, which needs openpyxl, and the project environment does not have it. The converted model writes the same table as `output/CONCRETE_FRAME_CREEP_AND_SHRINKAGE_PERIOD_RESULTS.csv`, with the time column and the period columns added. The source plots its periods but does not export them. The figures are HTML files: the curves and histories of each run (`vis_08_curves_*`, `vis_09_history_*`), the periods of each run (`vis_10_period_*`) and the deformed shape (`vis_05_deformed_steel01.html`). The model writes `CONCRETE_FRAME_CREEP_AND_SHRINKAGE_PERIOD.json` for the Hysteretic run's model. The solver prints `Strain Compression Limit Exceeded` for each fibre beyond its compressive limit, from day 203 on: the source's two runs printed 568,030 such lines, and the converted script prints 852,085 over its three runs.
12. **Not carried over.** The same unused code as in `SalarDelavar_Creep_And_Shrinkage_Analysis`, Note 10 (the commented uniform load `dl`, the commented recorder block, the `TDConcreteEXP` settings, and the strain and stress values that no material call uses, including `eps1C`, `fc2C`, `EtC`, `Bs` and `Esh`). `py_ref/` keeps all the source's Python files.
13. **Other sub-models.** The third sub-model, `CREEP_AND_SHRINKAGE_SENSITIVITY_ANALYSIS_REBAR_Cdepth`, is not converted here.
